# User interview guide

45 minutes. Semi-structured. Goal: evidence about **specific past incidents**, not opinions about a hypothetical product. Don't show Nexus until the final section.

## 0. Setup (3 min)
- Thanks, consent, recording permission.
- "There are no right answers. I'm trying to understand how you work, not testing you or pitching anything."

## 1. Context (5 min)
- Walk me through a normal workday. Where does information arrive? (Email accounts, calendar, WhatsApp, Slack, documents.)
- Roughly how many emails need a real response from you each week?
- Who else touches your calendar? (Recruiters, assistants, colleagues sending invites.)

## 2. Critical incidents — changes & conflicts (12 min)
- Think about the **last time** you showed up at the wrong time, wrong place, or with the wrong information. What happened?
  - How did the information differ? Where did each version come from?
  - When did you find out? How?
  - What did it cost you? (Time, money, reputation, stress.)
- Last time a plan **changed** — flight, meeting, deadline. How did you find out? Did anything downstream need updating?
- How often does something like that happen? *(Anchor: last week? last month?)*

## 3. Critical incidents — commitments & gaps (10 min)
- Think of the last thing **you promised** someone over email ("I'll send it tomorrow"). Did it happen on time? How did you remember?
- Last time **someone else** promised you something and it didn't arrive. How long before you noticed?
- Last time someone asked you for a document (signed form, invoice, ID). How did you know whether you'd sent it?

## 4. Current workarounds (7 min)
- What do you do today to catch these things? (Re-reading, flags, to-do app, assistant, a daily briefing, TripIt, nothing.)
- If you use an AI briefing (Gemini, CC, Pulse, Copilot): what does it catch? What has it missed? Has it ever told you something wrong?
- What would you **never** want an assistant to do on its own?

## 5. Trust (5 min)
- Would you let a tool read your email and calendar to point these things out? What would make that feel safe? What would make you revoke access?
- If a tool told you "your interview may be on the 5th, not the 6th" — what would you need to see to believe it?

## 6. Concept reaction (3 min, only at the end)
- Show the Nexus demo conflict card and a briefing-style paragraph describing the same facts.
- Which would you act on? Which do you trust more? Why?
