# Problem statement

> **Status: hypothesis.** Not yet validated with users. See [research plan](../research/research-plan.md).

## The problem

Personal information that matters — when an interview is, what time a flight leaves, what you promised a client, whether you sent the signed form — is spread across email, calendar and documents. Each source is individually accurate *at the moment it was written*. The failures happen **between** sources and **over time**:

- Two sources disagree and you only look at one (calendar says the 6th, the recruiter's email says the 5th).
- Something changed and the old version is still the one in your head (or your calendar).
- You promised something in an email and nothing reminds you.
- Someone asked for a document and you can't tell whether it was ever sent.

## Why retrieval isn't enough

Search, chat-with-your-inbox and memory products answer *"what does my data say about X?"* — but only if you already know to ask. The costly failures are the ones you **didn't know to ask about**.

## Why a daily briefing isn't enough (hypothesis)

Briefings (Google CC, ChatGPT Pulse) are narrative and ephemeral. They tell you what's on today. They don't:
- hold two sources side by side and say they disagree,
- keep an unresolved item alive until *you* resolve it,
- show how sure they are.

## Who has it most (hypothesis)

People with high volumes of **external, time-bound coordination**: job seekers mid-process, founders and consultants, recruiters, frequent travellers.

## Problem statement (one sentence)

> People who coordinate with many others over email and calendar miss conflicts, changes, open commitments and unconfirmed requests that span their sources — not because the information is missing, but because nothing compares the sources and keeps unresolved items visible until they're resolved.

## How we'd know we're wrong
- Diary study shows < 1 meaningful situation per person per fortnight (A1).
- People with briefings report they already catch these (A6/A9).
